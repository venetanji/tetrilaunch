# Tetrilaunch 1.0.6 — release plan

Tracking document for the 1.0.6 release. The PR that carries this file is the
release's task list; the sections below are filled in by the review and
planning passes and checked off as the work lands.

## Scope

- The double-gameplay UX: front door + tier hub redesign (PR #223).
- Everything merged to `staging` since `v1.0.5`: Workshop plates, Steam Deck
  controller parity, /about landing page, Steam packaging and store plan, the
  fullscreen Settings toggle, the promo capture harness, the phone-browser
  fullscreen gesture, the wind notch, and the copy rulings from #223's review.
- Three new music masters, shipped as **alternates** — a second song under
  `menu`, `bay-1` and `bay-2` rather than three new roles (#225).
- Store screenshots regenerated from the shipped build for every listing size
  (`sim/promo/run.ts --shots`), plus the promo montage beats.
- The 1.0.6 adversarial review (`docs/reviews/1.0.6/`, eleven lanes) and the
  fixes for what it found blocking: the lesson card no longer graduates the
  school, the post-ladder salvage faucet is back, and a macOS signing failure
  can no longer drop the Play upload (#234).

Everything in the technical change list below is on `staging`. Nothing is open
against it but this branch, which merges last.

## Release notes (draft)

Paste-ready for the GitHub Release body. **Not** for the store "what's new"
fields: this draft is ~5,000 characters, Play's field takes 500 and Apple's
4,000. The store text is the **What's new** block in `docs/PLAY.md` (one text,
both stores), and `npm run store:copy` length-checks it against Play's cap.

### Play

- Flight School is an offer now, not a gate. Press Play and you can take the
  lessons or go straight to Tier 1; either way you fly licensed.
- Quitting a bay drops you back at the tier hub — the Workshop, today's
  Contracts and the tower are one button away instead of a screen away.
- A finished tier waits for you. Land the run, clear the Contracts, then press
  **Unlock** when you want the ride: the next Mark opens on your say-so.
- On a phone browser, the first press of Play takes the game fullscreen. It is
  the one gesture a browser will accept the request from, so it is the one the
  game asks on.

### Hub

- The menu is two screens: a front door that just starts the game, and a tier
  hub that holds the run, the Contracts and the Workshop as three cards, each
  with its own button.
- The tower fits all eleven floors without scrolling, and the Flight School
  lobby is gone from it — the ladder is the ladder.
- A square **!** marks whatever is new since you last looked. Press the thing
  it is sitting on and it goes out, and stays out.
- Today's Contracts sit on the hub as three chips you can start from where you
  are standing.
- Three screens get a second song. The lounge and the first two bays each flip
  a coin between two tracks, so the music you hear most is the music that
  repeats least.

### In the bay

- The wind is a small tab under the bay banner instead of a gauge drawn across
  the middle of the field. It shows which way the wind pushes and how hard, the
  Weather Survey's average, and one word for the status — no number, and
  nothing over the airspace your shots fly through.
- The tab's colour is picked from the wind's strength and runs amber to red. It
  never goes green, because green is the colour of **STAB** sitting a few pixels
  away in the same row, and a gale wearing the "you are fine" colour is the
  misread worth designing out.

### After a run

- Scores read as numbers: **12,345**, not 12345. The end card and every board
  group their figures, and so does the money on the card beside them — a
  grouped score above a bare five-figure total reads as a typo in one of the
  two.
- The end card's row is shorter. The tower is the one door back to the hub,
  which is where the Contracts, the Workshop and the run all live; it carries
  the **!** when there is something waiting on it.

### Workshop

- The shop is one panel instead of a shelf you scrolled. Every system is a
  plate, grouped by where it is — in the rack, in the shed, on the shelf — so
  you can read your whole rig at a glance. On a short landscape phone a fully
  stocked yard can still run a little past the bottom of the plates region;
  everywhere else it fits without moving.
- Pick a plate and the detail panel says what the next tier actually does, in
  numbers, instead of a price and a name.
- A padlock means you cannot have it. A system you are already flying is never
  locked and never dimmed, even when its next rung is over your Mark's budget —
  the budget says so itself.
- Tier 3 says out loud that it is a refit-stop part, bought with scrap on a
  run, not with salvage here.
- A price you cannot pay answers with a card that says what is short, instead
  of a dead button that says nothing.

### Desktop & Steam

- On a controller, **A** clears the tier-unlock banner instead of launching a
  run behind it.
- The pad scrolls a panel before it leaves it, so the Owned/Installed strips
  and any gated card below the last button are reachable without touching the
  screen.
- The bay banner and the "letting the bay settle" note scale with the field, so
  a docked Deck or a high-DPI monitor no longer shows two low-res islands in a
  crisp HUD.
- Riding the elevator to Flight School rolls the licence mark onto the plate,
  not "-2".
- Fullscreen is a Settings toggle on desktop rather than a third button on the
  field (F11 and ⌃⌘F still work, and the toggle follows them).

### Fixes

- The top tier is claimable again: finishing the last Mark's two halves now
  opens the seal phase instead of quietly doing nothing.
- The Workshop panel no longer scrolls sideways by 4px, and the top row's
  badges are not clipped by the panel edge.
- The promo page and its gallery are not shipped inside the game any more —
  about 1.6 MB of marketing left every Android, iOS, desktop and Steam build.
- The desktop build cannot open a web checkout for in-game content; the store
  door is closed in the shell by construction, not by an unset key.
- The leaderboard screenshot no longer reads "No scores yet": the capture
  presses the App's own button, so the rows are fetched before the shutter.
- The Full Game preview marks the Skydeck as **earned**, not bought — it is
  unlocked by flying, and the panel said otherwise.
- "Play again" is spelled one way. The hub's cards said "Play again" and the
  two end cards said "Play Again"; sentence case wins, and the pin that guards
  it reads the source so a seventh spelling cannot appear on a screen no
  fixture renders.

## Risk list

**Save format — one new field, and one READ that had to be narrowed.**

The only new field is `acked`, and it arrives with **#223**: `meta.ts` defines
and defaults it, `lib/store.ts` validates it on load.

**#233 changes no field and is the more serious of the two,** because it was
losing data rather than risking it. `loadMeta`'s rule for grandfathering a
1.0.5 licence promoted a save that had "walked the old on-ramp — a system
installed, or a run filed". The on-ramp became a ladder with the Workshop rung
between lessons 4 and 5, so owning a system stopped meaning "finished the
school" and started meaning "in the middle of it" — and a save standing at
lesson 5 came back from `loadMeta` graduated, lessons 5 to 9 gone, silently.
Measured on `staging` before the fix: licence 4 in, licence 10 out.

The rule now needs a filed run. **What it costs, stated rather than hoped:** a
1.0.5 save that held the old licence and bought a system but never filed a run
keeps its count instead of graduating. It is not stranded — the ladder puts it
on the Contract rung, which `schoolContractDone` already reads as done, and
then the Workshop rung, which reads the system it already owns — so it is
carried forward without a press. Lessons re-offered beat lessons deleted, and
only one of the two is silent.

**THE PIN IS A SAVE/LOAD ROUND TRIP**, walking every rung from 4 to 9, because
nothing smaller can catch this class of bug: every surface reads the licence
correctly and the save writes it correctly, and the loss happens in between. It
needs the player to CLOSE THE GAME, which is exactly why a sitting at the
device never met it — and the reason to re-read the QA pass below with restarts
in mind rather than trusting one continuous session.

*(This paragraph read "`meta.ts` and `lib/store.ts` are unchanged between
`v1.0.5` and `staging`, verified: `git diff … is empty`" until the train ran.
That was true when it was written — #223 was not yet merged — and false the
moment it landed; the diff is now +181 in `meta.ts` and +18 in `lib/store.ts`.
The claim it was making is the one above, which the train did not change. Kept
as a note because a release-page audit that cites a command a reader can run
has to cite one that still returns what it says.)*

- `acked?: Partial<Record<NextStepId, string>>` — optional, defaults `{}` in
  `newMeta()`. `loadMeta` reads it off `raw`, drops anything that is not a
  plain object of strings, and needs **no migration**: absent reads as "nothing
  acknowledged", which is true of every pre-1.0.6 save. It fails *lit* (one
  extra glance at a button), never dark. **Low risk.** Confirm the follow-up
  commits keep that defensive read, and that `NextStepId` values are only ever
  written as keys (a renamed step id orphans an entry; harmless, but it re-lights
  the badge once).
- `NextStepId` gains `"unlock"`. A save written by 1.0.6 and loaded by a 1.0.5
  build carries `acked.unlock` as an unused key — `loadMeta`'s spread keeps it,
  nothing reads it. **Forward-compatible.**
- **Deferred claim is a real behaviour change to banked state, with no schema
  change.** `tierRunDone` / `tierContracts` now sit true-and-unclaimed until the
  player presses Unlock. A save caught mid-tier by the update is *correct* — it
  simply arrives on the hub with the Unlock button lit. **Verify** that a save
  which finished both halves under 1.0.5 (and so already advanced) does not
  light Unlock again: `tierReady` requires `tierRunDone && tierContracts >=
  TIER_CONTRACTS_REQUIRED`, and `advanceTier` resets both — so a 1.0.5 save is
  already reset. **This needs one explicit test with a real save, and it has
  not been run.** #223's own re-validate column asked for it; no 1.0.5 save
  exists on the test phone, so the step was skipped. It is the single test
  that would have caught #233 and the lesson-card graduation before the review
  did. Release-blocking — see the QA pass.
- `completeOnboarding` writes `licence = SCHOOL_FLIGHTS` in one go. Idempotent
  and monotone; a player who climbed the ladder is already there. The
  `celebratedMark` / `licence` grandfathering in `loadMeta` is untouched.

**Scores and economy.** No D1 migration is pending: `migrations/` still holds
`0001`–`0003` and nothing was added since `v1.0.5`. README calls `DELETE FROM
scores` the one irreversible step in a release — **1.0.6 does not need it.**
Tiers, `budgetForMark` and the board key are unchanged, so 1.0.5 scores stay
comparable. The only economy-adjacent change is *when* the Mark advances, not
what anything pays: the salvage shares are still banked by the recorders, and
`claimTierUnlock` pays only the award's rounding remainder. **Do not clear the
board.** If the merge train's re-validation shows a payout difference, stop —
that would make it a score migration and change the release's shape.

**Routing.** The largest behavioural surface. Home splits into front door and
tier hub; the Flight School lobby is removed from the tower; quit-from-run and
the coach-fail card's Menu now land on the **hub**, while Settings / How to Play
/ the hub's own back still go to the **front door**. Risks: a back-stack that
strands a player on the front door mid-tier, pad focus (`focusInitial` parks on
`.btn--primary`) landing on the wrong card, and deep links / resume paths that
assumed one menu screen. `sim/uifit` fixtures and the chromium baseline were
already updated on the branch (874 lines of `baseline.json`), which is the
signal to read closely rather than rubber-stamp.

**Native build inputs.** Unchanged by this release, and that is the claim to
check rather than assume: `ios.yml` passes `MARKETING_VERSION` from
`app/package.json`; `android.yml` takes `versionName` from the tag and
`versionCode` from `github.run_number`; `desktop.yml` runs `check:version` with
`RELEASE_TAG` before anything expensive. The `closeBundle` plugin that strips
`about/**` runs on the `NATIVE_MODES` set only — a change to the mode list would
silently put 1.6 MB back into every package, so `verify:store` and a package
size glance both belong in the QA pass. `verify:store:desktop` must stay green
for the Steam depot tree.

**Since #234 the sentence above is out of date in the right direction.**
`check:version` with `RELEASE_TAG` now runs in **all three** release workflows,
not desktop alone, so a tag that disagrees with `app/package.json` fails on
every platform before anything is signed. `android.yml` takes the `v*` tag
itself instead of being dispatched from `desktop.yml`'s `package` job, so a
macOS signing failure cannot silently drop the Play upload any more. `ios.yml`
archives an iOS privacy manifest and asserts it is in the archive.

**Tap targets — the one 1.0.6 regression the fleet cannot show you.** The hub
tower fits eleven floors without scrolling because `.tower__floor` divides the
shaft by eleven. On `main` the floors measured 37–42px; on this tree they
measure **21–43px** — 13 of the 23 fleet rows under the house `--tap-min` of
44px, every phone row under 33px, worst `108×21` on iPhone 13 mini. The trade
is argued in `app.css` (`.tower__floor`'s header, "the accepted cost of a
building read at a glance") and the `::after` hit zone buys 2px the rectangles
do not record, so this is accepted, not a crack. But it is invisible in the
numbers we gate on: `baseline.json` grew from 162 to 235 entries to absorb it,
so "235 baselined, 0 new" is true and says nothing. The QA pass below eyeballs
the tower on a phone for that reason.

**Two things the train taught, worth keeping.**

*A base change does not re-run CI.* #228 was opened against `main`, which would
have broken release step 3's `git merge --ff-only staging` and shipped real code
to production without a playtest. Retargeting it to `staging` left its five
green checks attached to the unchanged head sha — green against the **old**
base. Since it lengthens a user-facing string, the fleet was re-run against the
merged-onto-staging tree before the merge (235 baselined, 0 new). **Any PR
retargeted from now on is unverified until something re-runs against the new
base.**

*A transform scales paint, not reveal.* #229's first pass drew the wind fill as
a cyan → amber → red gradient revealed by `transform: scaleX()`. `scaleX` scales
an element's *paint*, so every width squashed the whole ramp into itself and a
0.08 breeze rendered red at its tip — every strength drawing the same picture.
Codex caught it as a P2 and was right. The colour is chosen from the magnitude
now, and the ramp has no green leg because `--success` green is the **STAB**
word a few pixels away in the same 13px row.

**#220 is closed** — the *earlier* Workshop plan (Option A, master–detail).
`docs/workshop-redesign-plan.md` already landed on `staging` through #222 with
the Option A3 plan the canvas review settled instead. It reported `dirty`: it
would have conflicted with the file that superseded it, replacing a 333-line
executed plan with a 209-line rejected one.

**#221 rode, and the web path is still the one thing unverified by hand** —
its author did not hardware-test the branch where the on-field button is kept.

**What is left** is step 5: this branch onto the post-train `staging` (merged
forward through #234), after the playtest and the real-save load test.

## Release steps

1. Merge the train above into `staging`. `staging.yml` deploys the staging
   Worker (its own D1, `tetrilaunch-leaderboard-preview`) on every push.
2. QA on staging (next section). Do not tag anything that has not been played.
3. Fast-forward `main`:
   ```bash
   git checkout main && git merge --ff-only staging
   ```
   Nothing deploys on this push — `staging.yml` is branch-filtered to `staging`
   and `android.yml` runs on the tag, not on `main`. **A PR is not enough for
   this step:** GitHub's merge button cannot fast-forward (a merge commit or a
   rebase both give `main` a sha `staging` does not have, and the tag would then
   sit on a commit nobody played). It is a local `--ff-only` merge and a push
   to `main`; if `main`'s protection refuses the push, it takes an admin
   bypass, not a PR.
4. Tag and push:
   ```bash
   git tag v1.0.6 && git push origin v1.0.6
   ```
   The tag then drives, in parallel:
   - **`desktop.yml`** — `check:version` with `RELEASE_TAG=v1.0.6` first, then
     Windows / macOS / Linux packaging in the `desktop-build` environment and a
     GitHub Release with the artifacts. It does **not** upload to Steam: that is
     a separate `workflow_dispatch` with `steam_upload` ticked, and it goes to
     the `playtest` beta branch only. Promotion to the default branch stays a
     manual click in App Admin.
   - **`android.yml`** — the signed `.aab`, published to the Play **internal**
     track. Promotion past internal stays human. Since #234 it runs on the tag
     in its own right, so a macOS failure in `desktop.yml` no longer cancels it.
   - **`ios.yml`** — archives with the manual App Store profile and uploads to
     **TestFlight**. App Store submission stays a click in App Store Connect.
5. Production Worker is manual and stays manual: run `production.yml` by
   dispatch (protected environment, required reviewer). It serves
   `tetrilaunch.com` off the same build.
6. **No `db:migrate` and no board clear this release.** `migrations/` is
   unchanged since `v1.0.5`; `DELETE FROM scores` is the irreversible step and
   1.0.6 does not earn it.

**What "verify" means here**, because one word covers four unrelated things:

| Command | What it actually asserts |
|---|---|
| `promo:verify` | **Reproducibility** between two full shoots (A/B), not a store rule. DOM screens byte-identical; gameplay scenes pass at tolerance 8 while the cannon/plant-frame sub-pixel drift is unexplained (see step 2 — it is not the sprite-bake crop) |
| `verify:store` | The RevenueCat SDK survived tree-shaking into `dist/`, **and** no `test_` key reached a shippable bundle. Guards a silent failure: a build with the env var unset lets Rollup prove the branch dead and drop IAP entirely — the app still builds, still runs, and simply has no purchases in it |
| `verify:store:desktop` | The inverse, for a Steam depot — no purchase surface, `isDesktop` gate present |
| `store:copy` | The Play listing text in `docs/PLAY.md` against Play's field limits, by code point. The Console truncates over-length copy at paste time rather than refusing it |
