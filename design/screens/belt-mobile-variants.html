Conveyor belt — three phone fixes

Every frame here lays out at 844×390 — a phone in landscape, the exact box game/layout.ts solves for one — and is then magnified with a transform. That is the whole point: the belt scales with the field, but the NEXT label and the shipment tiles stop scaling at their max() floors (7px font, 26px tile). Draw the page any bigger than 1:1 and both bugs politely disappear. The belt chrome inside each frame is app.css verbatim, so these are not an impression of the bug — they are the bug.

All three versions fix both problems. The lift — the fix for the belt sitting low — is identical in all three; they differ only in what they do about the caption. B shipped, with the caption dropped rather than relocated: its frame below is the belt that is now in the build, animating and lit by its cargo, not a picture of one. A and C stay as the record of what else was on the table.

  • B moved the caption into the tread, where no tile could reach it. What shipped goes one step further and drops the words altogether, keeping only the small arrows — a caption that has to be squeezed to survive is a caption the layout has outgrown. The transport carries the meaning now: chevrons marching at the cannon, in the colour of the shipment on the belt.
  • The chevrons are cut into the plates, not painted on them. Two stacked gradients make the recess — black fading out downward for the shaded upper wall, cargo colour fading in downward for the lit lower one — over a bevel of two drop-shadows, sunk to 55% so the lit wall reads as a cut rather than a lamp. The housing loses the hard black drop the rest of the chrome wears: on a rotated element it hung off the low edge as a black border under the belt. Both tiles are opaque now and sit on top of the transport: dimming the uphill one to 65% was meant to say “this one is behind”, but a translucent tile reads as unfinished rather than as behind — the field’s left wall showed straight through its cubes, exactly where that tile sits.
  • Colour is the readout. --belt-c is set from the same source as the tile’s own cubes (theme.ts’s shipmentColor), so cryo reads cold and slag reads dead on the machine as well as the cargo, and “what’s coming” is legible from the belt in peripheral vision. The housing keeps its own cyan, so even a dead-grey run still looks powered.
  • Motion is the direction, and the chevrons ride the plates rather than sliding over them. The transport is built on one unit — --slat, the pitch of a belt plate: the tread tiles at it, there is one chevron per slat, and both scroll right by exactly one slat per 900ms cycle. Measured subpixel, a chevron sits 0.01px off its plate’s centre and stays there for the whole cycle, at every viewport size.
  • The slat is world-space (37 world px), so the plates scale with the belt they belong to — the tread’s old fixed 20px tile meant 5 fat plates on a phone and 11 thin ones at 1440. The rollers turn once per 2850ms, which is not a taste call but the time a roller of that circumference takes at the speed the belt is actually running. Everything stops under prefers-reduced-motion.
